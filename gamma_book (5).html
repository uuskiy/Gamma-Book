Chapter 01
What is Gamma? Gamma is the "accelerator." It measures how fast an option's Delta changes as the market moves. High Gamma means the price of the option will explode or collapse very quickly.
Chapter 02
How does it work? It's all about Dealer Hedging. To remain neutral, Market Makers must buy or sell Futures against the options they've sold to you:

Positive Gamma (>0): Dealers sell rallies and buy dips. This creates stability and slow-moving ranges.
Negative Gamma (<0): Dealers must buy rallies and sell dips. This creates volatility and violent, fast price action.

Chapter 07 — Core Rules
FUNDAMENTAL MARKET BEHAVIOUR WITH GAMMA AS CONTEXT
Premise
Positive Gamma (>0): Dealers sell rallies and buy dips. This creates stability and slow-moving ranges.

Negative Gamma (<0): Dealers must buy rallies and sell dips. This creates volatility and violent, fast price action.
Definitions
Gamma Wall = The longest bar in the histogram → Magnet. The strike with the highest absolute Gamma concentration. Price tends to gravitate toward it.

Call Wall = The strike with the highest net positive Gamma → Resistance. Dealers short calls sell the underlying as price approaches, capping upside.

Put Wall = The strike with the highest net negative Gamma → Support. Dealers short puts buy the underlying as price approaches, cushioning downside.

HVL (High Volatility Level) = Inflection point of the gamma exposure curve → Regime boundary.
Price above HVL → Mean Revert Bias
Price below HVL → Breakout Bias
Keep this in mind, it's very simple...
Positive Gamma Regime = Mean Reverting
Negative Gamma Regime = Breakout
